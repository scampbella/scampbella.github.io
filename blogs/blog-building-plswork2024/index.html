Building PLSWORK2024

January 02, 2024 8 min read

Building a PC is a rite of passage for anyone serious about computers. After months of research, comparing benchmarks, and hunting for deals, I finally pulled the trigger on what would become my daily driver: PLSWORK2024. I named it half as a joke and half as a prayer that it would actually boot on the first try.

The Part Selection Process

I knew I wanted something that balanced performance with power efficiency. An SFX build was the perfect answer. The Ryzen 7 7800X3D was the obvious CPU choice, with its 3D V-Cache delivering incredible gaming performance while sipping power compared to Intel's offerings. For the GPU, the RTX 4070 Super hit the sweet spot for 1440p gaming and could handle local LLM inference with its 12GB of VRAM.

One of my favorite decisions was going with an SFX power supply and a compact case. The entire system draws around 300W under full load. For context, that's less than many high-end GPUs alone draw these days. It's quiet, cool, and fits perfectly on my desk without dominating the space.

The Build Experience

Building in a compact case is always a puzzle. Cable management becomes critical when every millimeter counts, and you learn quickly that the order of assembly matters far more than in a full-tower build. I had to install the CPU cooler bracket before the motherboard went in, route the front panel cables before the PSU, and somehow manage to not drop a single screw into the abyss.

The moment of truth came when I pressed the power button and everything lit up on the first try. No POST errors, no debug LEDs, just a clean boot straight into BIOS. There's something deeply satisfying about seeing a machine you assembled yourself come to life. PLSWORK2024 has been my reliable companion ever since, handling everything from late-night gaming sessions to compiling code and running local language models.
